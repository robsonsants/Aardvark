# Results — run `resultados_2026-08-31_v2`

> Current source of truth for every number in this package. Produced after the defect
> described in `CHANGELOG.md` §1 was fixed. Anything dated before 2026-08-31 in this
> repository underestimates the method, because it was measured with test-file evidence
> being silently discarded.

**Scope.** 7 upstreams · 21 forks (top 3 active divergent per upstream) · 17 CVEs ·
51 fork×CVE verifications · 41 ground-truth pairs, of which **40 conclusive**
(33 positive, 7 negative) and 1 ambiguous.

---

## 0. The two research questions, answered

The mapping between the questions and the metrics is **crossed**, and it is easy to get
backwards: reliability answers RQ1, coverage answers RQ2.

### RQ1 — How reliable is automatic verification of patch propagation?

Against the automated ground truth, over the 40 conclusive pairs:

| | TP | FP | FN | TN | P | R | F1 | acc | κ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| union of the layers (adopted) | 30 | 2 | 3 | 5 | **0.938** | **0.909** | **0.923** | 0.875 | **0.590** |
| A+B rule (`--regra-ab`) | 30 | 0 | 3 | 7 | 1.000 | 0.909 | 0.952 | 0.925 | 0.778 |

**Answer: reliable enough to be useful, not reliable enough to be autonomous.** Roughly
nine in ten propagated fixes are recognised, and two verdicts in forty are false
assurances — the severe error, since a false positive declares a vulnerable fork safe.
The confidence intervals (§4) are wide enough that no configuration can be claimed
superior to another at this sample size.

### RQ2 — How much propagation is there, and how does it vary across the ecosystem?

**Mean coverage 61.5%**, with the variation across categories mattering more than the mean:

| Category | Verifications | Patched | Vulnerable | Uncertain | `FILE_NOT_FOUND` | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| support | 6 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **100.0%** |
| server | 12 | 11 | 1 | 0 | 0 | **91.7%** |
| client | 12 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 3 | **50.0%** |
| sdk | 21 | 7 | 2 | 5 | 7 | **19.4%** |
| **total** | **51** | **33** | **3** | **5** | **10** | **61.5%** |

**Answer, with a caveat that is itself a result:** coverage is high where the fork tracks
upstream closely and collapses in the SDK category, where forks diverge structurally.
But **the coverage metric mixes propagation with inheritance** — see §6. Of the 33
`CORRIGIDO` verdicts, only about 10 are propagation in the strict sense.

## 1. Where the verdicts stop

`FILE_NOT_FOUND` is **10 of 51 verifications** and is reported as a first-class outcome,
not as a footnote. It means the patched file no longer exists at that path in the fork's
HEAD — the upstream restructured (monorepo migration, moved file) between the fix and the
fork's current state. Fixed-path verification cannot see through that, and saying so is
part of the answer to RQ2: **reach into the fork is a ceiling on any HEAD-based method.**

The uncertainty zone (5 pairs, all in the SDK category) is a design decision, not a
failure: an ambiguous pair escalates to human audit instead of being forced into a binary
verdict. What it costs is measured in §3 and §4.

## 2. The human oracle

`auditoria_manual.py` — 41 pairs, single reviewer, two complete passes, three verdicts
revised on the second with a written justification checked against the code.

**Agreement between the automated ground truth and the reviewer: 36/41 = 87.8%**
(7 pairs involve non-corrective probes).

Against the **human** labels, over the 40 conclusive pairs:

| | TP | FP | FN | P | R | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| union | 30 | 2 | 3 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 |
| A+B rule | 29 | 0 | 4 | 1.000 | 0.879 | 0.936 |

**A finding about the union worth stating precisely:** its confusion matrix is *identical*
under both oracles, even though the two oracles disagree on 5 individual pairs — the
labelling errors cancel out in the aggregate. For the A+B rule they do not: of the three
cases where the rule acts, the reviewer confirms two as genuinely vulnerable and judges
one as already patched.

**So the claim "the A+B rule eliminates false positives at no cost" does not survive the
human oracle.** Under the human labels it buys precision 0.938 → 1.000 at the cost of one
true positive, decided on 3 cases in 2 forks. What it also does — and this cuts the other
way — is produce the **best Kappa under both oracles** (0.778 automated, 0.717 human) and
the best accuracy. Both readings are true; §4 explains why neither is significant.

**Declared threats.** Single reviewer, therefore no inter-rater agreement. Two complete
passes with three revisions. Few decisive cases. Closing this gap is the highest-value
open item in `REVIEW_RESPONSE_AND_ROADMAP.md`.

## 3. State-of-the-art baselines, all on this run

Recall measured against the **33 `CONFIRMED_PATCHED`** pairs (see `CHANGELOG.md` §4 for
the denominator bug this corrects):

| Approach | Reproduced in | Recall |
|---|---|---:|
| whole-file hash (Wyss et al. 2022) | `rw1_wyss2022_hash/` | 0.091 |
| patch-presence test, dual reference (PPTFI 2024) | `rw3_pptfi2024_dualref/` | 0.758 |
| AST + edit distance, single reference (VERCATION 2025) | `rw2_vercation2025_ast/` | 0.879 |
| **union of the layers (this pipeline)** | — | **0.909** |

**The union beats the best single layer by one pair** (30 vs 29 of 33). The direction is
the expected one and the mechanism is understood — L2B catches removal patches that L2A is
blind to — but one pair at n=33 is not a robust margin, and §4 shows the difference is not
statistically distinguishable.

### Declaration scope (rw6): it depends entirely on which oracle you ask

`rw6_patchlens2026_hunk/` reproduces the transferable mechanism of PatchLens (FSE 2026):
map the hunk to its AST subtree and compare **inside the declaration that contains the
patch**, instead of across the whole file. Measured on this run under **both** oracles:

| Oracle | Scope | TP | FP | FN | TN | P | R | F1 | κ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| automated | file (pipeline) | 30 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 |
| automated | **hunk** | 31 | **0** | 2 | 7 | **1.000** | **0.939** | **0.969** | **0.844** |
| human | file (pipeline) | 30 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 |
| human | **hunk** | 29 | 1 | 4 | 6 | 0.967 | 0.879 | 0.921 | 0.630 |

**Under the automated oracle it dominates** — it removes both false positives *and*
recovers a true positive, which nothing else measured here does. **Under the human oracle
it does not.** It makes the same trade the A+B rule makes: precision up (0.938 → 0.967),
recall down (0.909 → 0.879), F1 flat (0.923 → 0.921). What survives both oracles is the
Kappa gain (0.590 → 0.844 automated, → 0.630 human).

**One pair explains the whole reversal:** `tchapgouv/matrix-rust-sdk` on CVE-2024-40648.
The automated ground truth labels it vulnerable; the reviewer labels it patched — it is one
of the five pairs where the two oracles disagree. Declaration scope refuses that verdict.
Against the automated label that is a false positive removed; against the human label it is
a true positive lost. **The entire "it dominates" claim rests on one contested label.**

That is the argument for a second annotator stated as concretely as it can be: a single
disputed pair decides whether a mechanism looks like a clear improvement or like an even
trade.

**It also brings a failure mode of its own:** in **8 pairs the patched declaration does not
exist in the fork at all**, so there is no hunk to anchor on. That case has to be mapped to
a verdict deliberately, not by accident.

Reproduce:

```bash
python trabalhos_relacionados/rw6_patchlens2026_hunk/run.py --run resultados_2026-08-31_v2
python trabalhos_relacionados/rw6_patchlens2026_hunk/run.py --run resultados_2026-08-31_v2     --oraculo humano --refazer-metricas
```

## 4. What the validation experiments show (E1–E6)

Run: `python experimentos_revisores.py --run resultados_2026-08-31_v2`. All offline, both
oracles, ~40 s.

### E1 — The trivial baseline

Always answering `CORRIGIDO`, on a population that is 82.5% positive.
**Figures below are under the human oracle**; the trivial classifier, Layer 1 and the
union have an identical confusion matrix under both oracles, while Layer 2A, Layer 2B and
the A+B rule do not (under the automated oracle: 2A κ 0.533, 2B κ 0.441, A+B κ 0.778).

| Method | FP | P | R | F1 | κ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| trivial (constant classifier) | 7 | 0.825 | 1.000 | 0.904 | **0.000** |
| Layer 1 (literal comparison) | 0 | 1.000 | 0.091 | 0.167 | 0.034 |
| Layer 2A alone | 3 | 0.903 | 0.848 | 0.875 | 0.377 |
| Layer 2B alone | 0 | 1.000 | 0.788 | 0.881 | 0.565 |
| **union** | 2 | 0.938 | 0.909 | **0.923** | **0.590** |
| A+B rule | 0 | 1.000 | 0.879 | 0.936 | **0.717** |

In F1 the union beats the constant classifier by **+0.019**. That is the uncomfortable
number, and it belongs in the main table rather than out of it. What separates the two is
not F1:

- **Kappa 0.000 vs 0.590** — the constant classifier has zero agreement above chance, by
  construction. This is exactly what Kappa is for.
- **False positives 7 vs 2** — the trivial classifier produces *every* false assurance
  available; the union produces 2; the A+B rule, none.

On a population this imbalanced, **F1 is the wrong metric**, and now that can be shown
rather than argued.

### E2 — Threshold cross-validation

Leave-one-upstream-out and stratified 5-fold, thresholds chosen on training folds only:

| Aggregated out-of-sample (human oracle) | P | R | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| thresholds learned on the training folds | 0.914 | 0.970 | **0.941** |
| fixed thresholds (0.80 / 0.05 / 0.60) | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 |

Three readings, all of which belong in the text:

1. **The thresholds are not overfitted.** Learned out of sample, performance is
   essentially the same, and what difference exists runs *against* the fixed thresholds.
2. **But they are not identifiable either.** Under the automated oracle all five folds
   select `thr_high = 0.60`; under the human oracle four of the five select `0.90` —
   opposite ends of the grid, decided by which oracle labels the training folds. At n=40
   the data does not determine the threshold; it is a policy choice, and the text should
   say so instead of implying derivation.
3. **The difficulty is concentrated in one upstream.** In the `synapse`, `vodozemac`,
   `matrix-authentication-service` and `element-android` folds the result is perfect with
   *any* threshold (P=1.000, R=1.000). Only the `matrix-org/matrix-rust-sdk` fold errs.

> **6 of the 7 negatives and 5 of the 5 errors are in `matrix-rust-sdk`.** The effective
> sample of the hard problem is **13 pairs, not 40**.

### E3 — Is patch size a usable discriminant?

The baseline row is the current pipeline, whose confusion matrix is the same under both
oracles, so the comparison below does not depend on which one is used.

| Discriminant | FP | P | R | F1 | κ | pairs moved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| none (current pipeline) | 2 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 | 0 |
| patch/file ratio < 0.05 | 0 | 1.000 | **0.424** | 0.596 | 0.205 | **18** |
| **sign of delta < 0 (guard A)** | 0 | 1.000 | **0.909** | **0.952** | **0.778** | **2** |

**Patch size is not usable.** To zero the two false positives it drags 18 cases into the
uncertainty zone and halves recall. The median ratio among errors (0.036) is even *smaller*
than among correct verdicts (0.048), but the separation is not operable.

**The sign of the delta is.** Both false positives have a **negative delta** (−0.0209 and
−0.0120): the fork is closer to the PRE-patch code despite high similarity to POST. That is
precisely guard A of the A+B rule, and it resolves both cases by moving two verdicts.

### E4 — Confidence intervals and paired tests

Bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, union of the layers, **human oracle** (the automated oracle
gives the same point estimates and a Kappa interval of [0.216 · 0.895]):

| Metric | Value | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---|
| Precision | 0.938 | [0.839 · 1.000] |
| Recall | 0.909 | [0.800 · 1.000] |
| F1 | 0.923 | [0.842 · 0.985] |
| **Kappa** | 0.590 | **[0.196 · 0.875]** |

Exact two-sided McNemar (human oracle): union × Layer 2A p=0.250; union × Layer 2B
p=0.688; **union × trivial classifier p=0.727**; union × A+B rule p=1.000.

**At n=40 no difference between configurations is statistically distinguishable** — the
difference from the constant classifier included. The Kappa interval runs from weak to
almost perfect agreement.

This does not invalidate the results. It invalidates a *kind of sentence*. "Our method
outperforms the baselines" is not supportable; "our method is the only configuration
measured that produces no false assurance, and here is the interval" is.

### E5 — Failure analysis

All five errors are in `matrix-rust-sdk`, all in Rust:

| Type | Fork · CVE | Diagnosis | Fixed by A+B? |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP | tchapgouv · CVE-2025-53549 | L2A says patched (sim 0.931) but delta **−0.021** | yes |
| FP | tchapgouv · CVE-2025-66622 | L2A says patched (sim 0.896), delta **−0.012** | yes |
| FN | Hywan · CVE-2025-66622 | both layers in the uncertainty zone (sim 0.660, δ −0.024); test file | no |
| FN | Hywan · CVE-2022-39252 | uncertainty zone (sim 0.650, δ +0.020) | no |
| FN | tchapgouv · CVE-2022-39252 | uncertainty zone (sim 0.643, δ +0.020) | no |

Two failure modes, and they are not the same kind of thing. **The false positives have a
clear signature** — high similarity to POST with a negative delta — and are detectable.
**The false negatives are the method working as designed:** similarity around 0.65 is
genuinely ambiguous, so the pair escalates to human audit. They are the declared cost of
the uncertainty zone, not bugs.

### E6 — Operating curve

Human oracle. Under the automated oracle the curve is flat from 0.85 upwards
(P 0.966 · R 0.849 · κ 0.576), so `thr_high = 0.95` reaches the A+B operating point only
under the human labels.

| `thr_high` | P | R | F1 | κ | |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0.50–0.60 | 0.892 | 1.000 | **0.943** | 0.553 | max F1, 4 FP |
| 0.80 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 | **adopted** |
| 0.90 | 0.967 | 0.879 | 0.921 | 0.630 | |
| **0.95** | **1.000** | 0.879 | 0.936 | **0.717** | |
| *trivial* | *0.825* | *1.000* | *0.904* | *0.000* | |

Two consequences. **0.80 is not the F1 optimum** — 0.50–0.60 is, with 4 false positives;
optimising F1 here pushes towards the permissive classifier. And **`thr_high = 0.95`
reproduces exactly the operating point of the A+B rule** (P 1.000 · R 0.879 · κ 0.717).
There are therefore two independent routes to the same result, which is a **competing
explanation** for the rule's effect and removes any claim that the A+B mechanism is
necessary — just as the 0.06 margin was already a competing explanation for guard B.

### Split by difficulty: the pooled number describes a mostly-trivial population

`python metricas_subconjunto_dificil.py --run resultados_2026-08-31_v2`

E2 showed the difficulty is concentrated in `matrix-org/matrix-rust-sdk`. Splitting every
metric by that boundary shows how much the pooled figure owes to the easy majority:

| Oracle | Classifier | Subset | n | TP | FP | FN | TN | P | R | F1 | κ |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| automated | union (pipeline) | **hard** (rust-sdk) | 13 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | **0.667** | **0.571** | **0.615** | **0.235** |
| automated | union (pipeline) | rest | 27 | 26 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| automated | union (pipeline) | pooled | 40 | 30 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 |
| automated | A+B rule | **hard** | 13 | 4 | 0 | 3 | 6 | 1.000 | 0.571 | 0.727 | 0.552 |
| human | A+B rule | **hard** | 13 | 3 | 0 | 4 | 6 | 1.000 | 0.429 | 0.600 | 0.409 |
| both | trivial | **hard** | 13 | 7 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0.538 | 1.000 | 0.700 | 0.000 |
| both | trivial | rest | 27 | 26 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0.963 | 1.000 | 0.981 | 0.000 |

**Three things follow, and all three belong in the text.**

1. **The easy subset is solved.** 27 pairs, P=1.000, R=1.000, κ=1.000 — and it contains
   exactly one negative. Any method, including the trivial one, scores ≈0.98 F1 there.
2. **The hard subset is where the work is, and the method is weak on it:** F1 0.615 and
   κ 0.235. The pooled 0.923 is mostly the easy subset showing through.
3. **On the hard subset the trivial classifier wins on F1** (0.700 against the union's
   0.615 and the A+B rule's 0.600–0.727). Only Kappa separates them, and that separation
   is real: the trivial classifier declares all 6 vulnerable forks safe, while the A+B rule
   declares none. This is the sharpest form of the reviewers' single-class objection — and
   the clearest demonstration that F1 is the wrong metric for this problem.

The honest headline is therefore not "F1 0.923". It is: **the ecosystem splits into a
majority that any method verifies and a minority that none of them verifies well, and the
contribution is being able to tell which is which.**

## 5. Threshold sensitivity

`python sensibilidade_regras.py --run resultados_2026-08-31_v2` →
`resultados_2026-08-31_v2/sensibilidade_limiares.json`.

The Layer 2A thresholds are robust by plateau. The sensitive parameter is the **Layer 2B
margin**, which governs how much is escalated to human audit: at margin 0.11–0.17 the run
gives P 0.968 / R 0.909 / F1 0.938 with coverage 60.7%, and at 0.18 the second false
positive returns. `ZSS_NODE_LIMIT` is deliberately not swept here — it decides *which*
metric is computed, so moving it requires reprocessing the ASTs.

## 6. Inheritance is not propagation

`dataset_temporal.py` (138 API calls) adds the dates the pipeline never persisted.

- **Median adoption lag: 268 days** (n=53 measurable; 31 rows flagged `lag_confiavel=nao`
  for a missing date or a negative lag — a known artefact of the "last touch" heuristic
  inherited from rw5).
- **In 31 of the 51 pairs the fork was created *after* the fix commit.** Those forks never
  carried the flaw; they inherited already-fixed code. 23 of them count as `CORRIGIDO`.
- Of the 33 `CORRIGIDO` verdicts, **only about 10 are propagation in the strict sense**.

Since RQ2 asks about propagation, **the coverage metric as defined mixes two different
phenomena** — and the same is true of coverage figures reported in the surrounding
literature. Caveat: `created_at` is the repository's creation date on GitHub, not the
divergence point, so 31 is a **floor**, not an exact count.

**Divergence does not predict coverage:** Pearson(behind_by, coverage) = −0.269 and
Spearman = +0.135 at n=21 — contradictory signs, no detectable monotonic relation. A
negative result, reported as one.

## 7. The A+B rule (optional, `--regra-ab`)

Two guards that **refuse** a doubtful `CORRIGIDO` rather than inverting it, sending the
pair to the uncertainty zone:

- **A)** Layer 2A only counts as patched when `delta >= 0`.
- **B)** Layer 2B only decides when `sim_2a >= 0.60` (`SIM_MINIMA_2B`).

Both come from the diagnosis of the false positives observed in the 2026-08-17 run: L2A
reading high on a monolithic file where the patch is tiny, and L2B deciding on the edge of
its margin between two very distant files.

| Oracle | Rule | FP | P | R | F1 | κ | Coverage |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| automated | union | 2 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | 0.590 | 61.5% |
| automated | A+B | **0** | 1.000 | 0.909 | 0.952 | 0.778 | 59.1% |
| human | union | 2 | 0.938 | 0.909 | 0.923 | — | — |
| human | A+B | **0** | 1.000 | 0.879 | 0.936 | 0.717 | — |

**Open caveats, all of which must appear wherever the rule is reported.** It was designed
by looking at the false positives of the 08-17 run, so it is not independently validated.
`SIM_MINIMA_2B = 0.60` is chosen, not derived. In this run the three cases where it acts
are all in `tchapgouv/matrix-rust-sdk`, a fork already seen — so this is a **temporal
replication, not validation on unseen forks**, which would require `--top 10` in a
robustness section. And E6 shows `thr_high = 0.95` reaches the same operating point
without the rule.
